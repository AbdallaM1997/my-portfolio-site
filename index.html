How I got here

From a Cardboard headset built for a university grade to safety training running on real sites. The thread through all of it: education first, then a marketing agency, and a game studio on the side.

  1. 2018

    The first game: Xenon Blade

    Before any client work there was a game made for its own sake. With ChillMaze I built a survival hack-and-slash: a blade-wielding robot against waves of machines, with upgrades banked between runs. I got it onto Google Play. It was where I learned to finish something and ship it. See the game

    • Unity
    • C#
    • Android
  2. 2019

    First job, still a student

    Arabtesting hired me while I was finishing my degree, on the strength of that game. I built an AR colouring book with Vuforia and OpenCV that turned a child’s scanned drawing into a live 3D model, and shipped a Cardboard VR animals app to Google Play, my first thing on a store. See the animal book

    • Unity
    • Vuforia
    • Google Cardboard
  3. 2020

    Graduation project: ADHD VR

    A virtual bedroom a child explores through a Cardboard headset, with tasks designed to surface attention and hyperactivity indicators for the adult watching alongside. Graded excellent at the Higher Technological Institute, and the moment I decided XR was the work I wanted to do. See the project

    • Unity
    • Google Cardboard
    • Stereoscopic rendering
  4. 2020–23

    Learning projects at an education publisher

    Nahdet Misr Publishing Group brought me in to make printed books do more. The AR science textbook came out of this, with a pendulum, a thermometer and a neuron building themselves on the desk off a page, along with pop-up story dioramas. It taught me to build for teachers and content staff, not just for developers. See the textbook

    • Unity
    • Vuforia
    • Marker tracking
    • Android
  5. 2021

    Evenings at a digital marketing agency

    Eye Advertising took me on part-time alongside the publisher. Agency work is a different discipline: booth games that survive eight hours of strangers, mobile AR feeding a PC over the local network, pharmaceutical clients who need it right the first time. Four years of doing both at once. See the booth games

    • Unity
    • Touch and kiosk
    • Arduino input
  6. 2023–25

    Running R&D, and safety training in VR

    Promoted to senior developer and R&D team admin at Nahdet Misr, coordinating developers, artists and content specialists. The work widened: 3D and AR for NEOM in Saudi Arabia, occupational-safety VR for construction crews, a browser physics lab, web 3D lessons for Rwandan schools. Alongside it, a contract with Gigaverse to build Kuwaitar to the Kuwaiti primary curriculum. See the PPE inspection

    • Unity
    • VR headset
    • Real-time 3D
  7. 2024

    A game studio, and a game on both stores

    ExaRoll Studio in Dubai asked me to lead development and design. Shara71, a tile-matching puzzle across the seven emirates, went from design document to the App Store and Google Play, with post-launch updates still going. See it live

    • Unity
    • C#
    • Android
  8. 2025

    Full-time at the agency

    Eye Advertising became the main job. Patient-education VR with branching scripts a medical team can edit, oncology VR for clinicians, web AR that runs from a QR code, virtual exhibition stands and a conversational 3D avatar. Owning delivery end to end, prototype to on-site. See the patient VR

    • Unity
    • Web AR
    • ScriptableObjects
  9. Now

    AR safety briefings and SkillBanks VR

    The two most recent: a wind turbine that stands up on a desk with every hazard pinned to the part it belongs to, and a VR soft-skills scenario where you sit at a table with colleagues who push back while trust and collaboration meters move with your answers. Both are the same idea the ADHD room started with: put the person inside the situation and let them handle it. AR safety briefings · SkillBanks VR

    • Unity
    • Meta Quest
    • Branching dialogue
